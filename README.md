# Duckling V3 simulation

MuJoCo model, Simulink balance controller, and a ROS 2 SLAM + YOLO navigation stack for a small Microduck-based biped. Everything here runs in simulation; nothing has been validated on hardware yet.

**Hardware-readiness software update:** see [`results/hardware_readiness/HANDOFF.md`](results/hardware_readiness/HANDOFF.md) for the measured-input workflow, timestamped sensor interfaces, weak/stale-localization stopping, reproducible checks and remaining physical blockers. Historical room metrics below predate this update; they have not been rerun on the modified ROS stack. The shield still fails the sampled operating-clearance gate, and the Microduck policy is still unsuitable for deployment on this duck.

> **V3 handoff revision (17 Nov 2026, branch `v3-handoff-fixes`, baseline tag `pre-handoff-baseline`).** Black PLA look, one clean bill, collision shapes for the new head and rear shield, per-part masses for 30% infill (provisional), honest pass/fail tests, a fixed planner and stop logic, and a second "realistic" sensor profile (USB colour camera + 8×8 ToF) next to the ideal depth camera. Per-item status: [`results/HANDOFF_SONNET_REPORT.md`](results/HANDOFF_SONNET_REPORT.md) (model, geometry, tests) and [`results/HANDOFF_OPUS_REPORT.md`](results/HANDOFF_OPUS_REPORT.md) (ROS 2, sensors). Cross-reviews: [`results/REVIEW_BY_SONNET.md`](results/REVIEW_BY_SONNET.md), [`results/REVIEW_BY_OPUS.md`](results/REVIEW_BY_OPUS.md). Results from before the fixes are kept in `results/pre_fix/`; new ones are in `results/post_fix/`. **Headline: the corrected model is less flattering.** Walk, obstacles and fall-and-get-up now fail in simulation (see Part D), and navigation with the realistic sensors is much weaker than with the ideal depth camera (see Part F).

**Credits and licenses.** The robot model is derived from Pollen Robotics' [microduck_rl](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck_rl) (MJCF under Apache-2.0; meshes under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, not included here: clone upstream and run `scripts/make_duck_v3.py`). The Simulink plant uses MathWorks' [mujoco-simulink-blockset](https://github.com/mathworks-robotics/mujoco-simulink-blockset) (BSD-3, patched as described below). Walking uses Pollen Robotics' pretrained policies from Hugging Face (`pollen-robotics/microduck-policies`, not included). YOLO training uses Ultralytics YOLOv8 (AGPL-3.0); trained weights are not included. Generated model XML is in `model/`; mesh files and copied upstream files are regenerated by the scripts.

Then, from `C:\dev\duckling_sim`:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\inspect_model.py microduck_rl\src\mjlab_microduck\robot\microduck\scene_walk.xml
```

- `inspect_model.py` is the PDF's Part 2 printout: 14 servos (5 per leg, 4 in the neck/head), 0.737 kg upstream, timestep 0.002 s, keyframes INIT / STAND / SIT / FOLD.
- `make_duck_v3.py` copies the upstream model to `model\` and writes `duck_v3.xml`. **The block at the top of that file is the only place you edit**: weighed link masses, the V3 added parts, servo gains from your bench test. It also adds a push point with `push_x` / `push_y` force inputs (last two actuators), sensors with Simulink-safe names (`gyro`, `accel`, `quat`, `torso_pos`, `q_<joint>`), and a `sim_start` keyframe.
- `stand_numbers.py` measures the model in its STAND pose and writes `model\stand_numbers.json`, which MATLAB reads.

**The redesigned V3 head and rear shield are in the model.** The print design is versioned in `design\`. To change it, edit `design\build_design.py` and run it (it reads the upstream original parts from `Downloads\Duckling_V3_Design_and_MuJoCo\duckling_v3\original_parts`, or wherever its argument/env points). `prep_v3_meshes.py` takes the three STLs from `design\modified`, cuts each head shell from about 400k to 20k triangles, converts millimetres to metres, and measures volumes. `make_duck_v3.py` then removes the upstream head shells, jaw and soft mouth and puts the V3 shells in their place. Run it once before `make_duck_v3.py`:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\prep_v3_meshes.py
```

What the V3 revision changed in the model:
- **Black PLA.** Every printed rigid part uses one `black_pla` material (rgba 0.025 0.025 0.025, restrained highlights) in MuJoCo and in the URDF. Servos, bearings, battery, PCBs and the TPU soles keep their own looks. The lens ring is black too; remove `noenoeil` from `PRINTED_RIGID_MESHES` to undo that.
- **One bill.** The thin tongue-like lip is gone. The lower shell's front is pushed forward into a short, rounded, black bill (`design\build_design.py`; the old part is kept as `duck_head_lower_v3_pre_mouthfix.stl`). The upstream jaw, soft mouth and old head shells that made a second mouth are removed. The `mouth_tip` site sits at the new bill tip, and picking is scored from it. Renders: `results\geometry_v3\` (front, side, rear, three-quarter, two mouth close-ups).
- **Collision shapes** (ground-contact model `duck_v3_gc.xml` only): simple primitives for the head dome, skirt, lower shell and bill, and three separate boxes for the open-U rear shield, so its open top, bottom and inside stay open. They are defined in `scripts\collision_proxies.json`. Check them with `scripts\check_collision_proxies.py` and `scripts\check_contacts.py`.
- **Masses.** The old global `PRINT_FILL = 0.9` guess is gone. Each V3 print has its own mass (block at the top of `make_duck_v3.py`), and every number is recorded in `model\mass_ledger.json`. The replaced upstream parts are subtracted and the full head and torso inertia is recomputed (no double counting, no clamping).

**Estimates you must replace with measurements** (top block of `make_duck_v3.py`):
- **Print masses, PROVISIONAL** (30% gyroid, 3 walls, 0.2 mm layers, 1.24 g/cm³ assumed): upper head 51.5 g, lower head 43.1 g (recomputed for the new bill), rear shield 32.3 g, about 127 g in total. These are engineering estimates, not slicer output. Slice the final parts, weigh them after removing supports, and type the scale values into `V3_PART_MASS_G`. Do not just change a fill factor.
- **Replaced upstream head parts:** 104.5 g, estimated from mesh volume at solid density. Weighing your complete head link (`MEASURED_MASS`) replaces this whole calculation.
- **Whole robot: 759.6 g in simulation** (was 789.2 g). This is not a weighed robot: it still uses upstream servo, electronics and battery masses. Build the installed mass ledger from the handoff (Feetech servos, UNO Q, HAT, battery, BMS, camera, ToF, wiring, fasteners) and weigh the real thing.
- **Rear shield position `SHIELD_POS` is a guess, and it now matters.** With collision shapes, the shield touches the legs while walking and stops the duck getting up from its back (Part D). Measure where it really mounts, and check leg and head clearance through their full range, before printing it.
- V3 head position: front flush with the upstream face, bottom at the upstream shell's bottom. `HEAD_SHIFT` nudges it. Check against the real robot.
- The servo model (kp 0.55 N·m/rad, 0.96 N·m limit) is upstream's Dynamixel XL330, not your Feetech.

Things that differed from the PDF:
- A `mass=` on an added geom is ignored, because every upstream body has an explicit `<inertial>`. The script now folds the V3 parts into the head and torso links' own inertia instead.
- The walk model only collides at the feet. When the duck falls, the body passes through the floor; that is expected, and "fell" is detected from torso height. The ground-contact model (`--tag gc`) has the full collision shapes.

## Part B: MATLAB + Simulink

One-time build of the blockset (downloads MuJoCo 3.3.6 and GLFW 3.3.7 into the blockset folder, then compiles 4 MEX files with MSVC):

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\MATLAB\R2026b\bin\matlab.exe" -batch "run('C:/dev/duckling_sim/matlab/build_blockset.m')"
```

Why a custom build script: release 4.x of the blockset ships no prebuilt `.mltbx`, and its own `build` needs `gmake`. `build_blockset.m` runs the repo's `install` and then calls `mex` directly.

The one source patch (already applied in `mujoco-simulink-blockset\src\mj.cpp`, function `initData`): the plant block always started from the model's zero pose, with straight legs. Three lines make it start from a keyframe named `sim_start` if the model has one. If you re-clone the blockset, re-add:

```cpp
int simStartKey = mj_name2id(m, mjOBJ_KEY, "sim_start");
if(simStartKey >= 0) mj_resetDataKeyframe(m, d, simStartKey);
```

Then in MATLAB:

```matlab
cd C:\dev\duckling_sim\matlab
startup_duck        % paths; run first in every session
duck_numbers        % PDF Part 4 hand calculations + PD and LQR gains
build_duck_sim      % creates duck_sim.slx from scratch (PDF Parts 6 and 7)
open_system('duck_sim')   % press Run: a MuJoCo window shows the duck balancing
run_tests           % PDF Part 8, all six tests, about 4 minutes
```

To push the duck by hand: set `push_F = 3` (newtons, forward, 0.05 s at t = 1 s) in the workspace and run. `balance_on = 0` turns the loop off.

What `duck_sim.slx` contains: MuJoCo Plant (0.002 s) → Bus Selector → Rate Transitions → `Controller` (atomic, 0.01 s) → optional `Extra delay` → Rate Transition → Mux with the push inputs → Plant. Inside `Controller`: the `tiltFilter` MATLAB Function block (complementary filter, α = 0.980), gains `K_TH` and `K_D`, ±0.25 rad saturation, added to the left ankle and subtracted from the right (the right leg's joints are mirrored).

Two blockset gotchas the script handles:
- Set the plant's XML through the parameter `xmlFileRel`. Setting the hidden `xmlFile` directly gets overwritten by `dummy.xml` at the next model update.
- Switching the XML several times in one session can crash the blockset's bus parser. `clear mj_initbus` before each switch avoids it.

`run_tests` now checks tilt, height, drift, settling, joint margin and sustained actuator saturation, and it **stops with an error if any assertion fails**. All of them pass (`results\post_fix\test_results.txt`). Caveats: this Simulink plant only collides at the feet, so it knows nothing about the head or shield touching anything; and the backward push margin is small, about 1 N for 0.05 s.

**The retune.** The PDF's rule (bandwidth 2·p, damping 0.7) gives 4.21·pitch + 0.260·rate. With those gains test 5 failed at every delay: one extra 10 ms sample and a 2 N push started a growing oscillation and the duck fell backward about 4 s later. `scripts\tune_gains.py` sweeps the two gains against 0 to 4 extra samples of delay and pushes in both directions:

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\tune_gains.py
```

Every pair with `K_TH` between 1.5 and 2.5 survives all of it; at 3.0 it needs `K_D` of 0.15 or more; from 3.5 up it starts falling at the larger delays, and 4.21 fails at 2 or more extra samples. I set the top of the safe band, `K_TH = 2.5`, `K_D = 0.20`. In `duck_numbers.m` that is `BW_FACTOR = 1.4` (bandwidth 1.4·p instead of 2·p) and `ZETA = 0.77`. The cost: the standing lean goes from 0.7° to 1.7° and the forward push limit drops from 5.5 N to 5.0 N; the backward limit improves from 1.5 N to 2.0 N. The ROS node and `push_test.py` use the same new defaults.

Plots: `results\post_fix\test2_stand.png`, `results\post_fix\test3_push.png`. Model renders: `results\geometry_v3\`.

**Why three now fail, checked with A/B runs** (`results\post_fix\ab_*.log`):
- The pre-fix model passes all eight under the same new criteria, so the criteria are not the cause.
- With the shield's collision shapes switched off, `obstacles` and `fall_getup` pass again. So the shield, at its guessed position, is the cause: a real design question to settle before printing.
- `walk` still fails without the shield. The corrected head and torso masses change the gait: the old model's head inertia was about 2× too small, so the old walk result flattered the robot. **What was tuned.**
- *Walk command.* The policy stands still below a command of about 0.2. 0.3 walks.
- *Obstacles.* At 0.3 the duck stalls on the 5 mm plank. `scripts\tune_obstacles.py` sweeps the walk command and servo stiffness: command 0.5 at the upstream stiffness clears all five planks; doubling the stiffness makes it worse. With taller planks it clears 20 mm and stops or falls at 25 mm.
- *Dance.* There is no dance policy. The routine in `m_dance` is choreography I wrote on top of the velstand policy's own command inputs (body roll and pitch, body height, four head joints, turn rate), on a 120 bpm beat. Edit the `choreo` function to change it.

**Caveats.** The policies were trained for the upstream Microduck with the BAM XL330 servo model; here they drive the V3 masses through the model's simple PD servos. With the corrected masses and the shield's collision shapes they **no longer** walk as before: this is the "needs retraining" outcome in the PDF's Part 9 table, not "walks as before". The masses are still provisional, and nothing here says anything yet about your Feetech servos.

## Part E: SLAM navigation with the head camera and depth

`scripts\slam_nav.py` puts the duck in a 4.4 × 3.2 m room it has never seen (two partition walls forming an S-shaped route, three boxes) and asks it to reach a magenta beacon it cannot see from the start.

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\slam_nav.py --gif
```

Add `--view` to watch, `--no-match` to switch scan matching off.

Sensors the algorithm uses: head camera RGB and depth (160 × 120, both rendered from one MuJoCo camera at the upstream camera position), gyro z, and the camera's pose relative to a level body frame (servo encoders plus IMU roll and pitch). Position and heading come only from the SLAM; MuJoCo's true pose is used for scoring.

The algorithm, written for this project:

1. **Predict.** Heading from gyro z; position from the calibrated walking speed along that heading. 50 Hz.
2. **Scan.** Depth image → 3-D points → keep those 3 to 30 cm above the floor → nearest hit per 2° of bearing. Floor-only directions count as free space.
3. **Match.** Try a small window of shifts (±7.5 cm, ±6°) around the prediction and keep the one whose scan endpoints land best on a blurred copy of the map. Move 60% of the way there.
4. **Map.** Log-odds occupancy grid, 2.5 cm cells: free along each ray, occupied at hits.
5. **Find the goal.** Magenta pixels in the RGB image, their depth, and the current pose give the beacon's map position (running mean).
6. **Plan.** Breadth-first wavefront over free space kept 12.5 cm from obstacles: to the beacon if known, otherwise to the nearest frontier (free cell next to unknown). 1 Hz.
7. **Drive.** Pure pursuit of the path as a twist command to the walking policy. The head sweeps left and right so the 75° camera covers about 190°.

| | With scan matching | Without (dead reckoning) |
|-|-|-|
| Outcome | Reached the beacon | Reached the beacon |
| Time | 48 s | 45 s |
| Stopped this far from the beacon | 0.34 m | 0.41 m |
| Position error, mean / max | 5.3 cm / 11.6 cm | 6.1 cm / 13.3 cm |
| Beacon position error | 15 cm | 9 cm |
| Path walked, falls | 6.3 m, 0 | 6.8 m, 0 |

Outputs in `results\slam\`: `map.png` (map, true path in green, estimated path in red), `slam.gif` (the map growing), `camera_rgb_depth.png` (one camera frame), `summary.txt`.

**What this does and does not show.**
- Scan matching helps only a little here. Over a 6 m route with a perfect simulated gyro and a calibrated walking speed, dead reckoning alone is already within 6 cm. The correction matters on longer routes and with real gyro drift, which this run does not test.
- One room, one start, one run each. No loop closure: the map is never re-aligned after the fact.
- The depth image is ideal (no noise, no holes), and the camera's height and tilt are taken as known exactly.
- It is a 2-D map from a 3-D sensor. Anything lower than 3 cm is treated as floor.
- This script runs in Python next to MuJoCo. Part F is the same algorithm as ROS 2 nodes, with YOLO added.
- **This standalone script was not updated in the V3 handoff revision.** It keeps its own copy of the old planner, including the bugs fixed in `ros2\duck_nav\duck_nav\duckslam.py` (a start area that could clear real walls, diagonal corner cutting, no map-bounds checks). Treat its results as pre-fix, and use the ROS package (Part F) for anything that matters.

## Part F: SLAM + YOLO as ROS 2 nodes, duck walking around a room

The package `ros2\duck_nav` runs the whole thing as six ROS 2 nodes in WSL. The duck starts in a 5.2 × 4 m room with three partition walls, five boxes and two 8 mm planks on the floor, explores it, walks to the beacon when YOLO finds it, then finishes exploring.

| Node | Job | Main topics |
|-|-|-|
| `world_sim` | MuJoCo room + duck + walking policy. Stands in for the robot and its sensors. Commands older than 0.5 s (sim) or 1 s (wall clock) are dropped, so a stopped publisher cannot leave the duck walking | in: `/cmd_vel`, `/head_yaw_cmd`. out: `/clock`, `/joint_states`, `/imu`, `/camera/image_raw`, `/camera/camera_info`, `/camera/pose_level`, and `/camera/depth` (ideal) or `/tof/zones` + `/tof/pose_level` (realistic) |
| `yolo_node` | YOLOv8-nano on the camera image (ONNX Runtime, CPU). Any image size: letterbox and mapping back. Newest frame only | in: `/camera/image_raw`. out: `/yolo/detections`, `/yolo/image`, `/yolo/stats` |
| `slam_node` | DuckSLAM: predict (tilt-aware heading), depth or ToF scan, scan matching, grid map; beacon position from YOLO boxes + range, only when the range is trustworthy | out: `/map`, `/slam/pose`, `/slam/state`, `/goal`, `/slam/beacon`, TF `map → base_level` |
| `nav_node` | Frontier exploration, path planning, pure pursuit, head sweep. Sends a zero command whenever it stops or hands over | in: `/map`, `/slam/pose`, `/slam/state`, `/goal`, `/nav/enable`. out: `/cmd_vel`, `/plan`, `/nav/status`, `/nav/result` |
| `score_node` | Referee: compares SLAM with MuJoCo's true pose at the same time stamp, writes the summary. Feeds nothing back | out: `summary.txt`, `result.json`, `map.png` |
| `robot_state_publisher` | Draws the duck in RViz from the URDF | |
| `usb_camera_node` | **For the real robot, untested.** UVC camera (Arducam B0385 OV9782 colour global shutter, or B0196 IMX219) → `/camera/image_raw` + calibrated `/camera/camera_info` | not used in simulation |

**Two sensor profiles**, chosen with `sensors:=`:

| | `sensors:=ideal` (default) | `sensors:=realistic` |
|-|-|-|
| What it stands for | A perfect RGB-D camera: the original simulation, an upper bound | What the V3 will actually carry: a USB colour camera and a VL53L5CX 8×8 ToF (Pololu #3417) |
| Range data | 320×240 depth image, perfect | 64 zones, 45° × 45°, 10 Hz, 2 m usable, ±1.5% noise, 3% zone and 2% frame dropouts, 60 ms latency. "No return" is never treated as free space |
| YOLO range | Median depth inside the box | ToF zones that project into the box, after correcting for the body motion between the two stamps. Must agree within 15 cm, otherwise the detection is reported as `unranged` and gives no goal |
| Mapping | 2° bearing bins, at least 3 points each | One bearing per zone column, 1 point is enough; scan matching needs only 4 hits |

The two are scored separately. Never compare a realistic run with an ideal one and call it the same thing.

**Outcome states** (`/nav/status`, final one on `/nav/result`): `exploring`, `heading for the beacon`, `escape` (backing out of the clearance margin over known-free cells), `waiting for observations`, `stale pose`, `stale map`, `slam recovering` (big tilt: translation and mapping pause, heading is still tracked), `robot is outside the map`, `goal rejected`, `exploration stalled` (no usable frontier, but the reachable room is not closed) and `exploration complete` (the reachable room is closed by mapped walls and at least 2 m²). Reaching the beacon is reported separately, from the true distance. A run that hits `timeout` is reported as TIMEOUT, never as success.

### YOLO: how the detector was made

A COCO-pretrained YOLO does not know what a beacon or a plain box in MuJoCo looks like, so I fine-tuned YOLOv8-nano on rendered images. MuJoCo's segmentation render gives the labels for free.

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install torch torchvision --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install ultralytics onnx onnxslim
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\worldgen.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\yolo_dataset.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\yolo_train.py
```

- `worldgen.py` writes the demo room (`model\duck_v3_gc_world.xml`) and makes random rooms for training.
- `yolo_dataset.py` renders 580 head-camera images from 45 random rooms, two classes: `beacon` and `box`. Pink and purple boxes are included as distractors.
- `yolo_train.py` trains 30 epochs on the CPU (about 13 minutes) and exports `yolo\duck_yolo.onnx`.
- On 72 held-out renders: beacon mAP50 0.995, box mAP50 0.954 (recall 0.90). These are synthetic images from the same generator, so this says nothing about real camera photos.

Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL-3.0 licensed, which matters only if you distribute a product built on it.

Drive it yourself instead: launch with `autonomous:=false` (or publish `false` on `/nav/enable`, which makes the navigator send one zero command and go quiet), then publish at 10 Hz or more, because a command older than 0.5 s is dropped:

```bash
ros2 topic pub -r 10 /cmd_vel geometry_msgs/msg/Twist "{linear: {x: 0.5}, angular: {z: 0.3}}"
```

`linear.x` is the policy's walk command, not metres per second. Measured in MuJoCo (`ros2\duck_nav\tools\calibrate_odom.py`, `results\post_fix\odom_calibration.txt`): forward 0.44 to 0.45 m/s per unit for 0.3 to 0.5; below 0.25 it stands still. Reverse only moves beyond about −0.4 (0.59 m/s per unit at −0.5, nothing at −0.3). Sideways is lopsided (+0.3 moves, −0.3 does not), so SLAM does not predict sideways motion.

|-|-|-|-|-|
| Exploration | stalled at 142 s (99% of the room mapped, 22 frontier cells left) | TIMEOUT, 66% mapped | TIMEOUT, 83% mapped | TIMEOUT, 72% mapped |

An earlier realistic run, before the last review fixes, reached the beacon with 0.25 m mean error (`*_realistic_v3model\`).

**What this says.**
- With an ideal depth camera the stack works well.
- With the camera and 64 ToF zones the duck will actually carry, it is **not reliable yet**. SLAM error ranges from 0.25 m to 1.2 m mean, the map drifts and doubles walls, and no realistic run finished exploring.
- When it reaches the beacon, that is partly from wandering, not from an accurate map (seed 2: the beacon estimate was 2.5 m off).
- Better localisation for sparse ranges (more scan-match constraints over the head sweep, loop closure, slowing down when the match is weak) is the next real work, before trusting navigation on the robot.

Pre-fix results (old model, old code, latest-message scoring) are in `results\pre_fix\ros2_nav\`. They are not comparable one-to-one.

### Things to know

- **RViz map display not confirmed.** RViz started and stayed up, but logged one shader error (`indexed_8bit_image.vert`), which is the shader the Map display uses. I did not look at the window, so the map may not draw. If it is blank, start RViz with software rendering: `LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1 ros2 launch duck_nav duck_nav.launch.py`.
- **There is still no loop closure**, and the realistic ToF profile's map drifts and doubles walls (see the results above). The handoff says it plainly: ideal-depth accuracy does not survive the switch to 64 coarse zones.
- **Heading through falls.** The heading now comes from the gyro projected on the filtered "up" direction, not raw gyro z, and it keeps integrating while the duck stumbles. Translation and mapping pause until the duck has been upright for 1 s. In a 40 s walk-and-turn test in MuJoCo the heading error was 2.6° with this, against 11.9° with gyro z. A real fall can still wreck the estimate, as the realistic run that fell shows; re-localising after a fall is not implemented.
- **Near walls, the duck can loop** between "backing out of the clearance margin" and its plan for tens of seconds, because pure pursuit cuts corners into the margin. It does not hit anything, but it wastes time. Not fixed.
- **"Exploration complete" is strict.** At 99% coverage the ideal run still reported "stalled", because 22 frontier cells were left. That is deliberate: the old code called a run complete after five empty plans, even on an all-unknown map.
- **Camera images.** In the ideal profile the camera does not see the duck's own body (as when YOLO was trained). In the realistic profile it does, so if the bill ever enters the view, YOLO sees it.
- **Planks are invisible to the map on purpose.** Anything under 3 cm counts as floor, and the walking policy steps over the 8 mm planks.
- **`/camera/pose_level`, `/tof/pose_level` and the body tilt are exact.** They stand for what neck encoders and the IMU would report; on a robot they will carry error.
- **The ToF's simulated optical origin is 12 mm ahead of the model's `tof` site**, which sits behind the head shell's front surface. The real sensor needs a clear opening: do not assume black PLA, or any dark window, passes its infrared light.
- One room in all runs. Not yet tried: other layouts, delays or sensor outages. Pre-fix runs are in `results\pre_fix\ros2_nav\`.

## Not done, and why

- **Servo bench test, weighing, ruler checks** (PDF Parts 3 and 5): need the physical robot. The model runs on upstream and example numbers until you enter yours.
- **Slicing and weighing the prints.** The 30% infill masses are provisional. Slice every final part, record the slicer settings and the support mass, weigh after support removal, and type the values in. Build the installed mass ledger, and do not count both the old Raspberry Pi electronics and the UNO Q/HAT that replace them.
- **Rear shield.** Not rebuilt (A3). Its mount position is a guess, and in simulation it hits the legs and stops get-up. Measure the real board stack, cables and standoffs, then re-check legs and head through their full range.
- **Bill fit.** The new bill is watertight, one piece and positive volume, but wall thickness, seam fit and internal clearance were not measured.
- **Camera and ToF hardware**: nothing bought or tested. Before freezing the head CAD, check on the real parts: board drawing and lens envelope, USB enumeration and streaming on the UNO Q (and whether a hub is needed), ToF power at 3.3 V logic (the Pololu carrier's pull-ups follow VIN), zone order, range convention (radial or along the boresight: `tof_range_convention`), and a rigid shared bracket. Then calibrate camera intrinsics and the camera-to-ToF extrinsics.
- **A ROS node for the real ToF**, and a hardware publisher for `/camera/pose_level` from the neck encoders and IMU. Its frame must be the MuJoCo camera convention (looks along −z, y up), while `usb_camera_node` stamps images in the REP-103 optical frame.
- **YOLO on real images.** The detector has only ever seen MuJoCo renders. Collect and label photos from the real black duck's camera (split by recording session), retrain, and benchmark the ONNX model on the UNO Q.
- **Simscape Multibody / URDF import in MATLAB, linearisation, margins** ("Industry Way" Steps 1 and 4): need toolboxes this MATLAB install does not have.
- **Retraining the walking policy** on your measured masses and Feetech servo model: needs an NVIDIA GPU (yours is AMD) or a rented one. With the corrected masses the pretrained walk fails its criteria (Part D), so this is now required, not optional.
- **Dependency pinning and a fresh-workspace build** (part of S8) are not done.
- **Anything on the UNO Q**: see `UNO_Q_GUIDE.md` for the step-by-step plan.
- **Roll (sideways) balance**: only pitch is controlled. The PDF's "repeat for roll" step is still open.
